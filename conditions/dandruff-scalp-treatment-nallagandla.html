Dandruff and Scalp Treatment in Nallagandla

Persistent flakes are not always simple dandruff. Itching, redness, thick scale or hair loss can point to a different scalp condition that needs a proper diagnosis.

Dandruff is common, but a scalp that keeps flaking or itching may also be affected by seborrhoeic dermatitis, psoriasis, contact dermatitis, fungal infection or another inflammatory condition. These can look similar at home and need different treatment. A dandruff and scalp consultation in Nallagandla begins with the pattern of scale, symptoms, triggers and the condition of the hair and skin.

Dandruff is not the only cause of flakes

Simple dandruff usually causes loose white or grey scale, often with mild itching and no marked inflammation. Seborrhoeic dermatitis can produce greasy scale and redness around the scalp, hairline, eyebrows, ears or nose. Scalp psoriasis often creates thicker, more sharply defined scale and may occur with psoriasis elsewhere on the body.

Contact dermatitis can follow a new dye, oil, shampoo or styling product and may cause burning as well as itch. A fungal infection is less common in adults but matters when there are broken hairs, tender areas, pustules or patches of hair loss. Repeatedly using the wrong product can prolong irritation.

How the scalp is assessed

The dermatologist asks when the flaking began, whether it is seasonal, which products have been used, whether the ears or face are involved, and whether there is pain, oozing or hair loss. The scalp is examined for scale, redness, broken hairs, pustules, plaques and signs of scarring.

Trichoscopy can magnify the scalp and hair shafts, helping distinguish inflammation, follicle changes and different patterns of hair loss. A fungal scraping, culture, blood test or biopsy is not needed for everyone, but may be considered when the appearance or response to treatment does not fit straightforward dandruff.

Treatment and recurrence

Treatment depends on the diagnosis and may include a medicated cleanser, topical treatment for inflammation, treatment of infection, or changes to an irritating product. The frequency and contact time of a shampoo can matter as much as the ingredient, and the plan may change between a flare and maintenance.

Some scalp conditions are controlled rather than permanently cured. Symptoms can return with stress, illness, weather changes, product exposure or stopping maintenance too early. A review is useful when a treatment helped briefly and then stopped working, because the diagnosis or the plan may need to be reconsidered.

When dandruff is accompanied by hair loss

Flaking does not automatically cause permanent hair loss. Scratching can break hairs, and inflammation can increase shedding, but sudden bald patches, painful areas, crusting or a widening area of loss should be examined. Scarring alopecia can permanently damage follicles, so early assessment matters when the scalp is painful, burning or changing quickly.

Hair and scalp concerns are assessed through the clinic's trichology service. The right sequence is diagnosis, appropriate treatment and monitoring rather than changing products indefinitely or booking a procedure before the cause is known.

When to see a dermatologist

Arrange an assessment when dandruff persists despite a reasonable trial of an appropriate product, keeps returning, affects sleep, spreads beyond the scalp, causes redness or sores, or is associated with hair loss. Bring the products and medicines you have already used so the consultation can start with a clear history.

Frequently asked questions about dandruff and scalp treatment

How do I know whether it is dandruff or psoriasis?

Dandruff often causes loose scale and mild itch, while scalp psoriasis may cause thicker, more defined plaques and can occur with psoriasis elsewhere. The appearances overlap, so persistent or severe symptoms should be examined.

Can dandruff cause hair loss?

Inflammation and scratching can increase breakage or shedding, but dandruff is not the explanation for every case of hair loss. Bald patches, pain, crusting or rapid change need a separate scalp and hair assessment.

Why does my dandruff keep coming back?

Many scalp conditions are controlled rather than permanently cured. Recurrence can follow weather, stress, illness, product exposure or stopping maintenance once symptoms improve.

Do I need a scalp test?

Not everyone needs testing. A fungal scraping, blood test or biopsy is considered when the history, examination or response to treatment suggests that a simple dandruff diagnosis may be incomplete.
